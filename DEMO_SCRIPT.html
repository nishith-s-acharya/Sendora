Sendora Demo Video Teleprompter

1. Introduction & Architecture Overview
0:00 – 0:45
🖥️ What to show on screen
Show Sendora Login page at https://sendora-frontend.onrender.com → Click "Continue with Google" → Lands on Dashboard.
🗣️ What to say word-for-word
"Hello everyone! My name is Nishith S Acharya, and today I am excited to present Sendora — a production-grade, distributed email job scheduler service and real-time monitoring dashboard.

Our tech stack is built entirely on TypeScript across both ends: Express.js, Prisma ORM, PostgreSQL, and BullMQ powered by Redis on the backend; React 18, Tailwind CSS, and Vite on the frontend.

Crucially, Sendora uses zero OS-level or node-cron jobs. Cron jobs cannot guarantee millisecond accuracy across distributed nodes. Instead, Sendora leverages BullMQ delayed sorted sets in Redis combined with PostgreSQL as the durable source of truth and Redis atomic sliding-window rate limiters."
2. Campaign Scheduling & Rate Limiting
0:45 – 1:45
🖥️ What to show on screen
Click + Compose Email → Fill Subject & Body → Drag sample-recipients.csv → Highlight ✓ 5 valid emails → Set Start Time (1 min ahead), Delay (2s), Hourly Limit (100) → Click Schedule.
🗣️ What to say word-for-word
"Let's schedule a multi-recipient outreach campaign to see the scheduling engine in action.

I'll click Compose Email, enter our subject and body, and upload our recipient list via CSV. Notice that Sendora's universal file parser instantly validates all email addresses and displays live recipient badges for all 5 contacts.

Next, we configure our dispatch parameters: a scheduled Start Time, a 2-second delay between individual sends to prevent sudden burst spikes, and an hourly limit of 100 emails per sender.

When I click Schedule Email, the backend atomically saves the batch to PostgreSQL, computes the delayed offsets, and enqueues individual delayed jobs into BullMQ."
3. Live Queue & Real-Time Sent Delivery Log
1:45 – 2:35
🖥️ What to show on screen
Open Scheduled Emails tab (show 5 jobs with yellow SCHEDULED pills and 2s timestamps) → Switch to Sent Emails tab (show rows turning green ● SENT every 2s) → Click row to open Email Detail Drawer.
🗣️ What to say word-for-word
"Here in the Scheduled Emails queue, you can see all 5 recipient jobs lined up with their target dispatch times, precisely staggered 2 seconds apart.

When the start time arrives, the BullMQ worker picks up each job atomically, reserves a rate-limit slot in Redis, and dispatches the email via our isolated Ethereal SMTP sandbox.

Switching over to the Sent Emails delivery log, you can see the jobs completing one-by-one in real time with green SENT badges.

We can click on any delivered row to open the Email Detail Inspector Drawer, reviewing the full email message body, recipient address, sender channel, and unique Job UUID."
4. Fault Tolerance & Server Restart Resilience
2:35 – 3:20
🖥️ What to show on screen
Schedule an email for 45s in future → In terminal kill the server process (Ctrl+C) → Restart server/worker (npm run dev) → Show that the scheduled job resumes and delivers with zero loss or duplicates.
🗣️ What to say word-for-word
"Now let's demonstrate Fault Tolerance & Restart Resilience. A critical requirement for any production scheduler is that jobs must survive unexpected server crashes without being lost or duplicated.

I have scheduled an email to send 45 seconds from now. Now, I will simulate an unexpected server crash by killing the process right here in the terminal.

The server is down. Now, let's start the process back up.

Upon boot, Sendora's Startup Reconciliation Engine automatically scans PostgreSQL for any pending or stuck jobs and safely re-enqueues them into Redis. As you can see, the job executed on time with zero lost jobs and zero duplicate deliveries."
5. Database Verification in TablePlus
3:20 – 3:55
🖥️ What to show on screen
Switch to TablePlus → Open EmailJob table → Point out rows with status SENT and populated sentAt timestamps.
🗣️ What to say word-for-word
"Let's verify the persistent state in our database using TablePlus.

In our PostgreSQL schema, we have four core relational models: User, Sender, EmailBatch, and EmailJob.

Looking at the EmailJob table, we can verify that every single recipient is recorded with its full subject, body, target time, and upon completion, its status is updated to SENT with the exact sentAt timestamp.

This guarantees that even if Redis cache is flushed, PostgreSQL maintains an immutable historical ledger of all outreach activities."
